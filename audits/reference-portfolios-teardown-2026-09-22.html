What all nine agree on

Before the differences — the four non-negotiables. Break any one of these and you're not in the set.

Agreement 01
The artifact is the argument

Every one leads with real, full-fidelity, real-data product UI. Not one leads with a process diagram, persona, or double diamond.

Agreement 02
Words are rationed

Short, declarative, written in the voice of a decision-maker — not a student documenting an assignment.

Agreement 03
Claims are verifiable

All route to third-party proof: release notes, App Store, company blog, acquisition coverage, a colleague's tweet, the live product.

Agreement 04
The type system is tiny

1–3 fonts, 2–8 sizes, site-wide. Hierarchy comes from weight, colour, rules and space — almost never from size.

Consequence 1 · your strategy

The question is not "how do I write better case studies." It's "how do I move work into the column that verifies itself?"

Every live URL, Wayback capture, named quote and press mention lets you delete a paragraph. Deleting paragraphs is how the site gets better.

Consequence 2 · do not skip ahead

A stage-four site made by a stage-one designer reads as empty, not confident.

You're at stage one. Carl is your model. Stages 3 and 4 show the destination — and remind you every paragraph is debt you're paying because the reader doesn't know who you are yet.

The five sentences that make it credible

Structure above. These are the moves — the single thing most worth stealing from all nine.

MOVE 01Names the internal opposition — and says he overruled it.

"…carried real risk, and not everyone was sure we should. I made the argument anyway."

MOVE 02Lets data contradict internal opinion — including possibly his own.

"When I looked at real Core and Enterprise data, it did not show the editor mapping to real-world usage the way internal sentiment assumed."

MOVE 03Documents a course correction he later reversed.

"We later removed the auto-open behavior… Early discomfort can look like failure if you react too quickly."

MOVE 04Footnotes his own metrics like a researcher.

"Anchored to full enterprise rollout 2026-04-06, measured over the 30 days following, against a 30-day pre-launch baseline. External cohort (all non-internal accounts)."

And on the chart: "Growth later in the period is shared with Automations, which grew from 20.2% to 40.5% of executions." He attributes part of his own result to someone else's feature.

MOVE 05Ends with a question he could not answer.

"Top of funnel softened later in ways I could not explain from the data I had, and that is the thread I would pull next… we designed mostly for the tenth session, not the first."

Title slide, slide craft, and why Slides at all
  • Title slide: black. Eight product app icons (not wordmarks) — Anthropic, Facebook, Code for America, Indeed, Square, Cash App, Peach. Name + "Selected work". Nothing else.
  • Black throughout so every screenshot reads as a lit window.
  • Caption: small, centred, top, sentence case, ends with a period.
  • Screenshots bleed off the bottom edge — cropped by the frame, not floating. That's what makes them feel like real windows.
  • Three layouts only: 1-up centred, 3-up phones, 1-up browser chrome.
  • Real data everywhere: $256.12, SPDR S&P 500 · Jan 28 2024 3:30 PM · $1.16. Nothing is Lorem.
  • Older work on period-correct devices — 2019 Indeed in an iPhone SE with a home button.
  • Why Slides: loads instantly, works everywhere, no maintenance — and the medium sets expectations correctly. Nobody opens a deck expecting a 3,000-word essay. Choosing Slides is itself a decision to be concise.

THE MOVE: GROUPING + DATING

Six patterns that hold across all nine — type, motion, personality, proof, curation, and sentence headings.

10.1 · Type scale discipline
Nobody exceeds 8 sizes

Median is four. Three sites cap at 24px or below. If your pages have more sizes than this, cutting them is the cheapest available upgrade.

10.2 · Motion budget — the striking absence
Nobody has…

Scroll-jacking · parallax · cursor follower · page-transition curtain · magnetic button. Not one of the nine.

Where motion appears

The correlation between seniority and restraint is not subtle.

10.6 · Sentence-as-heading

A noun heading tells the bucket. A sentence heading tells the conclusion.

Daniel, Carl and Tammy all use complete sentences where a portfolio would normally use a noun:

“I love building things for others.” / “Design is a team sport.” / “Keep learning.” (Daniel) · “Sessions became the product model” / “Code stayed visible because trust takes time” (Carl) · “Final designs and solutions” (Tammy)

A sentence is always more useful and is never harder to write.

What none of them do

Worth listing — these are the defaults a portfolio drifts into when nobody is paying attention.

None of the nine ship any of these
  • No double diamond, no “my process” diagram, no generic empathy-map, persona or journey-map filler
  • No skills bars, tool logos, or “Figma ██████░ 95%”
  • No stock photography, no abstract 3D blobs, no gradient mesh backgrounds
  • No testimonial carousel (Jakub has testimonials — as a static list, not a slider)
  • No “Let's work together!” with an animated arrow

  • No case study longer than its evidence justifies
12

Steal this: apply it to your site

You are at stage one of the gradient. Carl is your closest model. Fifteen moves, tiered by leverage vs effort — plus what not to copy.

Where you are

Work mostly is not publicly verifiable yet — so narrative has to carry it.

Your existing strategy work already picked stakes-based positioning and a Khiladipro / Getmega / SEDP flagship ranking. Most of this slots into that rather than replacing it.

Tier 1Highest leverage · lowest effort
1
Outcomes at the top, with a methodology footnote.Star

Where you have a real number, put it above the fold and footnote the window and cohort. Where you do not: “Shipped to production, September 2024” beats a vague improvement claim. The footnote converts a number from marketing into evidence.

2
A proof link on every card.Star

kpro.fit's registration page is live and matches the design — that's a LIVE ↗ pill on the Khiladipro card, doing the job Tammy's facebook.com/settings does. Audit each of the five projects for one publicly verifiable artifact: live URL, app store listing, press mention, Wayback capture. You already have Wayback evidence for SEDP.

3
Collect named testimonials — the single highest-value item on this list.Star

Two or three quotes with full names and real job titles from people who saw you work on Khiladipro, GetMega or SEDP — a founder, a PM, an engineering lead. Bottom of the relevant case study, not the homepage. Keep them short; do not edit them into marketing copy. A four-word quote from a CEO beats a polished paragraph from nobody in particular.

4
Credit your collaborators by name.

Jenny Wen does it eleven times in 27 entries. Your Khiladipro case study has no collaboration story — naming the engineers, the PM and the founder, and saying what each did, is the collaboration story, and costs nothing you were otherwise claiming.

5
Every card one-liner as a reframe, not a description.

Pass each of the five through the Carl test: does the sentence say what changed for a user, not what you made?

6
Add a booking link.

One line, next to your email — Vince's cal.com move. Trivial to add, removes a step.

Tier 2Medium effort
7
A “what I cut and why” section on the flagship.

Strongest credibility move available; costs nothing but honesty. Same for one reversed decision and one open question. Your notes say Khiladipro has no iteration story — what we cut may be the honest version of that section.

8
Organisational outcomes where you have no metric.

Zegzulka's “resulting in addition of more ICs to support the design” is the model. Did a design get staffed up, copied into another surface, change how the team worked, survive a leadership review, or become the default pattern? That is an outcome and needs no dashboard.

9
Caption every image the Leo way.

One sentence, user's side, sentence case, ends with a period. “Check your balance and review recent transactions.” not “Dashboard screen.” Your kp filmstrip and shot figures are the place to start — doing this will reveal which shots have nothing to say and should go.

10
Name your sections after interactions.

If a case study section cannot be named after the interaction problem it solves (empty-state, version-history, prompt-enhance) then ask whether it earns its place. A good editing filter for the kp chapters.

11
A dated changelog instead of a bare archive.

Jenny Wen's format beats Daniel's table for you: reverse-chronological spine, one sentence per entry, wider definition of what counts — shipped features, talks, writing, small tools, experiments. Notion notes, Wayback research, redesign experiments, AI-native workflow all belong. Ecometer and Mega Poker don't deserve flagship treatment but deserve to exist — in a dated stream they're two lines that help.

12
Cut your type scale to six sizes and audit against it.

Your notes already endorse the CH 06 ladder for kp; extend that discipline site-wide. The median across these nine is four.

13
Named convictions.

Three or four beliefs with an argument each, in the manner of Carl's CONVICTIONS or Tammy's “My super powers.” Gives an interviewer a phrase to repeat about you in a debrief — and generalises across every role you apply to.

Tier 3Worth considering
14
The deck as a second artifact.

A 40–70 slide black deck of your shipped screens, one user-capability caption each — sendable as a link, presentable in an interview. Different medium, different expectations; fast from assets you already have. Highest ratio of impression to effort on the whole list given how much shipped UI you have across Khiladipro, Getmega, Mega Poker and SEDP.

15
One demonstration, not decoration.

Daniel's shader hero and Ian's air hockey game work because they are the argument. Your note already says build operable demos rather than reaching for cream-and-serif — one real, operable thing beats any amount of scroll animation.

Explicitly do not copy
  • Carl's visual skin — dark, DM Serif, mono eyebrows, numbered rails. It's the look you already flagged, and his footer advertises the tool.
  • Daniel's near-zero-text case studies. Requires a body of public, watchable, shipped work. Take the naming discipline, not the word count.
  • Tammy's outbound-only strategy for recent work. Only works when the world has already written about it. You need narrative to do that job for now.
  • Joel's or Ian's no-portfolio sites. Stage-four moves. Made at stage one they read as empty.
