## 0. The verdict in four sentences

The portfolio proves execution and ownership well, proves craft unevenly, and does not yet prove judgment under disagreement, research-led decisions, or recent practice. Its most distinctive material is buried: a 13-state component system, a real-money ledger, a camera that scores a child from three metres, a chart that leaves its dashboard. Its most damaging problems are small and cheap: placeholder data and AI-rendered mockups presented as shipped work, on the pages that argue for attention to detail. The redesign direction on this branch is structurally right and, in the places that matter most, thinner than the pages it replaces.

## 1. Positioning

### Where the audit lands, and why I would not stop there

The audit proposes "a hands-on product designer who makes complex workflows easier to understand and carries them through to delivery with small teams." That is true and safe. It is also the positioning of several thousand designers. "Complex workflows" is a category, not a reason to remember you.

The pattern that is actually in the work is sharper. Every project is a system where a person is trusting the product with something that has consequences:

- **FACT** Getmega and Mega Poker: money entering, sitting on a table, and leaving, with GST and TDS in the arithmetic.
- **FACT** Khiladipro: a parent pays before installing, then a machine scores their child through a camera.
- **FACT** SEDP, Ecometer, Agrimarket: public statistics that a journalist or citizen will repeat.

And in every project the design work is state work rather than hero-screen work: the 13-state leaderboard card, the MTT lifecycle, the olympiad's waiting, error, and progress states, the three-wallet ledger, comparison across mismatched years. **INFERENCE:** this is your actual expertise, and it is far more marketable than "gaming" or "dashboards" suggests. It maps directly onto fintech, marketplaces, health, insurance, and any consumer product with a payment or an algorithmic verdict in it.

### Thesis

> Priyank designs the moments where a product has to be trusted with something that matters: paying, wagering, withdrawing, being scored by a machine, being measured by a statistic. He designs every state of that system, not just the happy path, and ships it end to end with teams of two to four, which is why two of his three freelance clients came back with a second brief.

**FACT check on the last clause:** CEDA rehired you for SEDP after Ecometer and Agrimarket. Khiladipro rehired you for the IPL campaign page. Mega Poker shut down (**MISSING**: the shutdown and the ban are asserted on the page without a source). "Two of three" is defensible; "every client" is not.

### Two things the thesis deliberately does not say

- **"Senior."** **FACT:** your only full-time title was Product Designer at Getmega, 2019 to 2021. The word "Senior" on the homepage is self-assigned. The audit says it is "reasonable if it matches how you represent your experience elsewhere." I would go further: the level claim needs an evidence base on the page, and today the strongest seniority evidence on the site (Getmega: second designer, team grown from 2 to 10, hiring pipeline, daily CEO reviews) sits fourth in the list behind an 11-minute read time. Either the Getmega story carries the title or the title should drop to "Product Designer" until it does. Decide this before writing copy.
- **"AI-native."** **FACT:** the only AI-building evidence is a WIP Chrome extension that links to a repository. Khiladipro is an AI product you designed for; it says nothing about how you build. Agree with the audit: demote the claim until there is a demo.

### What the thesis does for the two audiences

The audit spends a lot of its length on a founder/contract audience and a "how an engagement works" section. Your brief to me is competitive product-design roles. For that audience a single line ("Open to full-time roles and selected contract work") is enough. Do not build a services page; it dilutes the level signal a hiring manager is looking for.

## 3. Role of each case study

The rule: each study proves one thing the others cannot. Today two pairs overlap (Getmega and Mega Poker both lead with wallets; SEDP and Ecometer both show the same brand on the same chart types) and the rehire story is told everywhere.

**Khiladipro** should prove: *end-to-end ownership under a hard deadline, on a problem no pattern library covers.* The unique material is physical: a phone propped across a room, a permission a minor must grant, a verdict readable mid-movement, a parent's card before the install. Nothing else on the site can make this argument. It should not spend its length on generic "conversion → activation → participation" framing.

**Getmega** should prove: *what two years inside one product does to a designer.* Systems (the 13-state card), lifecycle design (MTT from registration to results), a research-informed retention system with the one measured outcome on the site, and the only team-growth evidence. It should stop trying to be a tour of 600 screens and stop leading with the wallet, which Mega Poker owns.

**SEDP** should prove: *making a dense system legible to someone who did not build it, and designing an output that leaves the product.* Information architecture, comparison as a workflow (redesigned, not invented: the old portal already had it, **FACT** from the old screenshot), and share templates with verified public use. This is your range project and your public-good project.

**Mega Poker** should prove: *judgment when the business brief and the user's interest are in tension.* The reframing of the brief and the leave-table moment are genuinely strong product decisions, and the ethical tension is the interesting part, not a liability, if the page states what the user gets and where the guardrails were. It should be half its current length and should not re-explain wallets.

**Ecometer & Agrimarket** should prove: *the origin of a client relationship and a real before/after.* The Agrimarket "before" (a paragraph and three red buttons, **FACT**) against the redesigned dashboard is the most dramatic transformation on the site. That is one image pair and one paragraph, not a case study. Fold it into SEDP as "how the engagement started" or into a compact suite section.

## 5. Biggest gaps

What a strong hiring manager would still not be convinced of after reading everything:

1. **That you have ever changed your mind because of evidence.** **MISSING** on every page: a user session, a test, a review, or a metric that altered a design. The lo-fi and mid-fi prototypes exist (**FACT**, linked), which means the raw material for at least one before/after exists too. This is the single highest-value recovery job.
2. **That you can influence people who outrank you.** **MISSING**: one instance of disagreeing with a CEO, PM, or engineer and what happened. Getmega had daily CEO reviews for two years; it is not credible that none of them changed a decision. Recover one.
3. **What you have done in the last fifteen months.** **FACT:** the selected work ends in 2025; the site says 2026; the hobby card says TBD. A recruiter's first question will be "what are you doing now?" The audit lists this under a "Currently" line. I would rank it higher: for a competitive role, an unexplained gap plus a self-assigned Senior title is a red flag combination.
4. **What actually shipped.** **MISSING** everywhere except CEDA's posts: every screen is a Figma export. No production captures, no note on what engineering changed, no "designed versus shipped" inventory. Kpro.fit and the CEDA portals are public; capture them.
5. **That the craft survives inspection.** **FACT:** the audit's credibility findings hold, and I found more (section 6). A manager who spots "Lorem ipsum" in a shipped-product image stops trusting the captions.
6. **That "Senior" is earned.** See section 1. The evidence for it is on the site but not in the reader's path.
7. **That research is part of how you work rather than something you would do next time.** One sentence in five projects.

The audit's "narrative" and "credibility" sections cover 1, 4, and 5 well. It under-weights 2, 3, and 6.

## 6. What feels weak or generic

Grouped by the kind of damage it does.

### Reads junior

- The About creed: "Design with empathy. Think like a product owner. Build with curiosity." (**FACT**, your own words.) These are values any graduate would list. Replace with three examples or delete.
- Ecometer page: emoji section headings, "Click any image to see it full size," and a role line reading "Product Designer, UX Designer, Design Lead" on a team with one designer.
- "Verified by absolutely no one" badge next to your name in the first viewport. Funny once; it is the second thing a recruiter reads.
- "P.S." and "P.P.S." on both contact sections, "type hire anywhere," "probably asleep." Individually charming, together they take up more of the first screen than your work does.

### Portfolio-template-like

- Homepage structure: portrait, tagline, marquee of logos, five identical cards, two testimonials, contact. The redesign of the case studies has not reached the page that decides whether anyone opens them.
- "Case study · 4 min," "01 / 05," "Fin." on every page, "An honest scorecard" as a chapter title on four pages.
- The "Shipped / What I'd measure / What I'd do differently" triptych closes every study. It was a good honesty device once. By the fourth page it is a formula, and the reader learns that the outcome section will never contain an outcome.

### Polished but shallow

- The redesigned Khiladipro and SEDP pages have the best typography on the site and the thinnest evidence. Each "decision" chapter is a rationale for the final screen with a skill tag beneath it ("Product framing · Information architecture"). The original Khiladipro has the fold comparison at 1440 versus 390, the three-reader lens, the nine-screen film with a sentence per screen, and the constraint-to-chapter mapping. The redesign compressed exactly the material a design manager reads for. Agree with the audit that the redesign's structure should be the foundation; disagree that it is currently the stronger page. Merge, do not choose.
- The three-column "journey" (Choose & pay → Set up & try → Compete & return; Read → Compare → Share) is decoration. Order carries no information the reader needs.
- AI-rendered device mockups (**FACT**: the files are named `ChatGPT_Image_Apr_22_2026...`) serve as the hero of the deployed Khiladipro page, the SEDP original, and Mega Poker, and the Khiladipro original captions one "The shipped product." An AI render of your design is not the shipped product. On a page arguing for attention to detail, a design manager who notices the render will notice the caption.

### Generic product-design process

- "Three fidelity passes: lo-fi to agree structure, mid-fi to lock layout, hi-fi to polish." Every designer says this. What the passes changed is the evidence; the passes themselves are not.
- "Peak-end rule, applied deliberately." Citing a heuristic is not a decision.
- "What I would validate" sections stand in for validation on every page.

### AI-written tells

The copy on this branch has a recognisable rhythm: two-beat headline pairs ("The technology worked. / The experience had to earn trust."), aphorism pull-quotes ("Payment UI is trust UI. Nothing on this screen is decorative." "The score is the interface." "This wasn't a funnel. It was a relay."), and triads everywhere. Each one is fine. Thirty of them across five pages read as generated, and your own rule against dashes is already fighting the same tendency. The fix is not to ban the device; it is to cut two of every three and let a specific fact stand where the aphorism was.

### Repetitive

- Both CEDA studies show the same brand, chart types, and share pattern.
- The Khiladipro rehire is told on the homepage card, the About timeline badge, the case study outcome, the testimonial caption, and the original's stat strip.

## 7. Homepage strategy

What a recruiter should understand, by time on page.

**10 seconds.** Name. What kind of designer, in one specific line (the thesis, cut to eight words). Level and availability. One piece of work visible without scrolling. Today the first viewport delivers a portrait, a joke badge, a generic tagline, keyboard-shortcut coaching, and a sound-permission toast. No work.

**30 seconds.** Three projects that each make a different argument, with role facts on the card: "Sole designer · 8 weeks · rehired," "Two years in-house · team grew 2 to 10 · DAU 15k to 25k (team outcome)," "Redesigned CEDA's flagship portal · charts now used in their public posts." Plus a credibility strip: IIT Guwahati, Getmega, CEDA at Ashoka, two rehires. The reader should be able to say what you are good at without opening anything.

**2 minutes.** One case study skimmed with disclosures closed: the problem, your role, one decision with its evidence, the outcome with its limit. Then the About page's timeline and one paragraph on how you actually work, with a current-status line that is true.

The homepage's job is to get the right project opened. It is currently doing personality first and proof second.

## 8. Recommended architecture

```
Home
  ├─ Positioning line · availability · resume / LinkedIn      (first viewport, with the first project card)
  ├─ Three flagship cards, each with a proof line              (Khiladipro · Getmega · SEDP)
  ├─ One secondary card                                        (Mega Poker)
  ├─ Credibility strip: education, in-house, clients, rehires, both testimonials placed next to their project
  ├─ Now: what you are building or working on in 2026         (one item, real)
  └─ Contact

Case study (one template for all four)
  ├─ Summary block above the hero: problem · role · team · duration · what shipped · strongest defensible result
  ├─ Two or three decision chapters, each: what I knew → what I considered → what I chose and gave up → who pushed back → what the artifact shows
  ├─ Evidence gallery for the rest of the screens (collapsed)
  ├─ Outcome, with its limit stated once
  └─ Next case study

About
  ├─ Timeline (already good)
  ├─ How I work: three specific episodes, not three values
  ├─ Now
  └─ Off hours (keep, below everything professional)

Removed or demoted
  ├─ Ecometer & Agrimarket page → merged into SEDP
  ├─ Compare page → not linked publicly
  ├─ Hobby "TBD" card → deleted
  └─ Sound prompt, unverified badge, "type hire" → off the first viewport or gone
```

One masthead, one navigation vocabulary, one reading width across every page. Product-specific accent colours can stay. Three visual systems on one site currently signals indecision, and visual taste is one of the five things you asked the portfolio to prove.

## Where I disagree with the Astra audit

For the record, so you can weigh two opinions rather than one.

- **Positioning.** "Complex workflows with small teams" is accurate and forgettable. The stakes-based thesis in section 1 is more specific, equally true, and travels better across industries.
- **The redesigns as foundation.** Right on structure, wrong on content. The redesigned Khiladipro currently has less decision evidence than the version it replaces. The merge is the work.
- **The founder audience.** The audit gives contract positioning and an engagement-process section a lot of room. For the goal you stated, that is a distraction. One line of availability is enough.
- **Recency and title.** The audit treats "Currently" as a copy line and "Senior" as acceptable. I treat the fifteen-month gap plus a self-assigned title as a combination that competitive roles will probe, and rank it in the top five.
- **Mega Poker.** The audit worries the "leak" framing narrows your judgment. I think the tension is the best product-thinking story on the site once it is told as a judgment call with the user's side stated, and the fix is reframing plus cutting, not softening.
- **Scope.** Ten prioritised fixes plus six per-page lists is a backlog, not a strategy. Five is enough to change the outcome.

What the audit got right that I would not soften: every credibility finding, the decision-episode structure (input, alternatives, decision, collaborator, evidence), the Getmega promotion, and the warning against backfilling research that did not happen.
