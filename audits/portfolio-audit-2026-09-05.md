# Portfolio audit — Priyank Agarwal

5 September 2026 · Positioning for midweight-to-senior full-time product design roles and freelance product design engagements

## Overall assessment

The portfolio contains credible material for an experienced, hands-on product designer. Your strongest evidence is ownership of complex workflows in small teams, interaction detail, and repeat client engagements. The current presentation makes the freelance execution case more readily than the full-time senior product designer case.

The seniority gap is principally an evidence gap: readers see considered final designs, but relatively little of how you established the problem, tested an assumption, negotiated scope, changed a decision, or influenced how the team worked. That is a judgment about what the portfolio demonstrates, not a judgment that you lack those abilities.

Keep the redesigned Khiladipro and SEDP pages as the public foundation. Restore selected evidence from the originals. Give Getmega a more prominent role in establishing experience inside a product team. Correct contradictions between screenshots and narrative before investing in another visual redesign.

**Recommended positioning territory:** a hands-on product designer who makes complex workflows easier to understand and carries them through to delivery with small teams. Khiladipro supports physical/digital onboarding; SEDP supports data comprehension; Getmega supports product evolution and team collaboration. This is more specific and better evidenced than “end-to-end across mobile, web, and dashboards.”

## 1. Positioning: one identity, two clear ways to work with you

The homepage says “Senior Product Designer: 0→1 & Growth Stage,” then spends much of the introduction on end-to-end work, mobile/web/dashboards, AI-native workflows, AI coding tools, and business goals. Almost none of those phrases helps a reader distinguish you from another experienced generalist. Mobile, web, and dashboards are surfaces; they do not specify the kinds of complexity you handle particularly well.

Your dual audience is manageable on one site. You do not need two identities or an audience-selection gate. You need one credible expertise and two explicit routes to engagement.

**For recruiters and hiring managers:** make level, recent work, product ownership, collaboration, location, employment availability, and resume access easy to find. A recruiter can establish basic fit; the case study must then support the design manager's deeper assessment.

**For founders:** make clear which problems you can take on, what you own, how you work with their team, what gets delivered, and how to start. A founder currently gets reassurance from the eight-week solo engagement and rehire, but little information about what an engagement with you would actually involve.

**Suggested homepage copy:**

> Priyank Agarwal — Product Designer
>
> I make complex products easier to understand and use.
>
> I work with small teams on consumer apps and data tools, from shaping the experience to the details that ship. My work spans sports-tech onboarding, economic dashboards, and payment flows.
>
> Based in Bengaluru. Open to full-time product design roles and freelance engagements.

“Senior Product Designer” is also a reasonable title if it matches how you represent your experience elsewhere. Avoid publishing “midweight-to-senior”: use it as an internal search range. Do not add an inflated leadership title to compensate for missing evidence.

Suggested action hierarchy: **View selected work** as primary, **Discuss a role or project** as secondary, with a visible resume link. Within contact, provide short “Hiring for a role” and “Planning a project” descriptions leading to the same inbox, optionally with different email subjects.

For contract work, add a compact explanation of supported engagements: new product workflows, redesigns of existing experiences, and complex dashboards. Explain milestones, review cadence, engineering collaboration, and handoff only as they actually apply to your practice. Do not promise a universal eight-week turnaround based on one past project.

Move substantive evidence from About onto the homepage: repeat clients, work alongside small engineering teams, and sustained in-house product experience. Add a truthful “Currently” line because the selected professional work ends in 2025 and the current page date is 2026. Do not fabricate a new project or require personal explanations for gaps.

## 2. Narrative: demonstrate how the answer was reached

The redesigns are stronger at explaining purpose. Khiladipro's conversion → activation → participation structure and SEDP's first use → compare → share structure are both useful. The limitation is that most passages explain why the final interface is sensible; they seldom show how competing possibilities were evaluated.

For example, “Lead with the event. Explain the technology later” is a plausible design rationale. It becomes stronger evidence of senior judgment when accompanied by the original brief, an alternate structure, feedback that influenced the choice, and the constraint that made the decision consequential.

Use this structure for two significant decisions in each flagship case study:

1. **Input:** what you knew, and whether it came from the client, research, existing behavior, technical constraints, or your own hypothesis.
2. **Choice:** what alternatives were actually considered.
3. **Decision:** what you chose and what you sacrificed.
4. **Collaboration:** which partner changed or challenged the direction, and how you resolved it.
5. **Evidence:** an artifact, review note, test observation, implemented change, or explicitly limited outcome.

Recover this from actual records. Do not backfill generic personas, fabricated user quotes, or a tidy process that did not happen. A small before/after pair with one honest revision is stronger than a decorative process diagram.

The word “sole” establishes responsibility but does not establish leadership. Show how your work made a PM or engineer more effective: a simplified release scope, fewer ambiguous states, a reusable pattern, an implementation compromise, a review that changed direction. Senior IC evidence need not involve direct reports.

Keep limitations, but stop giving the absence of analytics so much rhetorical weight. “I won't invent numbers,” “honest scorecard,” and similar formulations recur across the portfolio. One concise boundary is enough. Lead with what can be demonstrated, then state what was unavailable.

Distinguish four things throughout: a shipped output, observed use, a relationship outcome, and measured product impact. A rehire demonstrates client confidence. A social post demonstrates use of a template. Neither alone establishes conversion, usability, or growth improvement.

## 4. Visual hierarchy: make the work easier to inspect

The redesigns have stronger heading hierarchy and more coherent section rhythm than the originals. Both look considered. Their product-specific palettes help distinguish the work. Preserve those gains.

The main visual issues are allocation of attention and evidence legibility:

- The homepage's first desktop viewport contains identity, a long introduction, live status, shortcut instructions, and contact links, but no work example. The mobile opening similarly delays the work. Shorten the introduction and reduce vertical space above the first project.
- The featured Khiladipro card occupies most of a desktop viewport. Its short text does not expose role, duration, or ownership as efficiently as the image establishes visual style. Give those facts a clear line on the card and make it easier to scan the next featured project.
- On the redesigned case studies, the mobile role/team facts began approximately 1,130 pixels down for Khiladipro and 1,054 pixels down for SEDP in the tested viewport. Bring a compact facts-and-result summary above the hero artwork or directly under the introduction.
- Both redesigned mobile pages remained long with disclosures closed: roughly 10,900 and 9,900 pixels in this spot check. These are layout observations, not measured reading times. A “4 min read” should refer clearly to a skim path if inspecting artifacts takes longer.
- Large headings should identify a decision. Supporting labels should not require equal attention. Skill subtitles such as “Systems thinking · State design” can be useful metadata, but the evidence should do the main work of establishing those abilities.
- Keep one overview screen for context, followed by a legible crop of the exact detail under discussion. Whole desktop screenshots reduced into narrow columns or phones do not let someone judge labels, units, or control hierarchy.
- The SEDP before/after toggle saves space, but requires readers to remember one screen while inspecting the other. Add an immediately visible pair of matched, annotated crops. Retain the full-screen toggle as an optional inspection tool.
- In the mobile Khiladipro onboarding section, selecting a moment changes text while its product image is farther down. Put the selected visual immediately alongside or below the controls so the interaction's effect is visible.
- Establish a consistent personal masthead, navigation vocabulary, reading width, and contact pattern across homepage, About, new case studies, and older studies. Product-specific accent colors can remain.
- Remove the homepage's “Click anywhere to enable sound” prompt and make interface audio an explicit opt-in. Its current toast competes with contact links. Keep the playable instruments as an intentional About-page interaction.
- Demote the unverified-badge joke, command-menu coaching, “type hire,” and empty next-experiment card. Personality is useful; the combined set currently occupies too much of the attention needed for proof and fit.

No comprehensive contrast or accessibility failure is asserted here. A focused follow-up should check text contrast, zoom, keyboard navigation, reduced motion, touch access, and image-dialog behavior. Smooth/reveal animations were present; initial animation frames were not treated as permanent blank-content defects.

## 6. Differentiation: use the projects' actual specificity

Your work has a stronger pattern than the homepage expresses: helping people understand what a system expects, what it did, and what they can do next. That appears in a camera recognizing a body, a financial balance explaining available money, a chart retaining its context, and an event state offering the next action.

Make the portfolio memorable through those examples. “A phone three metres away,” “two indicators with different years,” and “a financial input hidden by the user's thumb” are specific design situations. “Think like a product owner,” “business goals remain paramount,” and “make complex things clear” need examples alongside them to become persuasive.

The AI claim is presently ahead of the evidence. Khiladipro is an AI-powered product, but designing its interface does not by itself demonstrate your own AI-assisted building workflow. The YouTube extension supports builder curiosity, yet is marked WIP and mainly links to a repository. If AI-assisted building is central to your current positioning, show a short working demo, a real user problem, one iteration, your contribution, and the limits of the implementation. Until then, keep it as a supporting experiment rather than a principal reason to hire you.

**What works:** distinctive physical context, explicit freelance role, clear team and eight-week constraint, concrete audience questions, the mobile parent-reassurance tradeoff, detailed onboarding stages, and a visible follow-up engagement. Its best material has more character and specificity than the compressed version.

**Main problem:** the story begins repeatedly. Hero, short version, numbers, brief, constraint register, experience map, then chapter 1 all retell overlapping context. Approximate extracted text is 2,964 words, including navigation and captions, versus 1,514 in the redesign. The long lead-in delays the most distinctive interaction work.

**Keep:** the connection between constraints and decisions, the physical setup context, selected state coverage, and the early summary of the outcome.

**Cut or condense:** the duplicated brief and numbers, numbered constraint cross-references, repeated claims about honesty, multiple closing takeaways, and excessive navigation between named chapters. “07 chapters” also conflicts with the visible six numbered chapters.

**Correct:** draft artwork, floor/elevated guidance, “payment field” versus basic-details screen, unsourced superlatives/traffic claims, and “every state” language when the reflection lists uncovered failure states.

**Do not restore the full original page.** Transfer its best evidence into the redesign's structure.

## How to evaluate the revised portfolio

Use a small formative review with people resembling your two audiences. The following are proposed exercises, not findings from this audit or claims about universal recruiter attention spans.

- **Brief homepage review:** ask what role you are seeking, what kinds of problems you solve, whether you accept contracts, and which project they would open first. Do not explain the site beforehand.
- **Short case study skim:** ask them to identify your responsibility, one consequential decision, the evidence for it, and the outcome. Repeat with disclosures unopened.
- **Deeper design-manager review:** ask what evidence would help them assess your seniority and what part of the story they would challenge in an interview.
- **Founder review:** ask what work they would hire you for, what they expect an engagement to include, and what uncertainty prevents them from contacting you.
- **Mobile check:** confirm project facts are easy to reach, chart details can be inspected, tab changes are visible, and the contact action is unobstructed.
- **Final factual pass:** distinguish contemporary project material, final designs, production captures, conceptual prototypes, and retrospective improvements. Verify quotes and public evidence links against the exact artifacts displayed.
