Transaction design Sole product designer 2024

Money moves. Clarity matters.

I designed the decisions around adding, moving and withdrawing money inside an existing poker product. From a balance in the wallet to a request in progress, the interface had to explain what the money meant.

The brief 00 / 05

One product. Different kinds of money.

Winnings, deposit and bonus carry different possibilities. The screens had to keep those distinctions visible, inside rules and a visual language that were already set.

The brief
More money remaining on-platform, alongside the redesign the product needed.
Style
A new visual language, already locked, which I applied alongside the old one.
Rules
The product’s existing rules, inherited as they were.
Outside it
KYC, which sat outside the withdrawal brief.

Withdrawal 01 / 05

The amount is only half the decision.

A player choosing to withdraw needs to understand the limits, the net amount and the alternative being offered. I brought those choices into the flow, before the request is submitted. The transfer offer encourages keeping winnings in the product: a different destination for the money, not the same outcome as cashing out.

Limits before input

The constraint is visible before an amount is entered.

Available winnings, the minimum, the tax-free allowance and the daily limits sit above a disabled Continue button. The amounts are sample content, not a statement of the tax rules.

The amount and the alternative

After submission

Received is not processed

A checkmark acknowledges the request. The tracker still has to say what is happening to the payment.

Three states, kept apart

The withdrawal brief also listed UPI and penny-check verification, fee and speed variants, and a TDS explanation sheet. Those were requirements; these exports do not show each one delivered.

The offer across contexts 02 / 05

Same incentive. Different money decisions.

The bonus offer appears at table exit, beside winnings in the wallet, and within withdrawal. The bonus mechanism came with the brief; I designed its presentation across those contexts, keeping principal and bonus as separate balances.

  1. At table exitCelebration, then the choiceProfit leads the sheet, followed by the incentive, the destination breakdown and the transfer action.

    Sample mismatch. ₹1,248.50 plus ₹250 is ₹1,498.50, not the displayed ₹1,508.50, and exactly 20% is ₹249.70. Shown unchanged, for its composition.

  2. In the walletBeside the winnings balanceDeposit and winnings group as playable balance, with bonus apart. The offer sits under winnings; withdrawal is signposted through the menu.

  3. Choosing the amountPrincipal and bonus, separatelyThree bonus tiers mark the slider, 10% selected; ₹8,100 and ₹810 take their own rows above the ₹8,910 total. A static selection example.

Three separate examples with different balances, not one continuous transaction. They establish placement and hierarchy; they do not establish who proposed each offer moment, or its effect on behaviour.

Deposits 03 / 05

A total is not a single balance.

“Pay ₹500, get ₹750” combines deposit money and locked bonus. I designed the amount selection and the breakdown to separate the charge, the adjustments and where each part goes. The exports do not establish whether every player opened or understood the summary before paying.

Sample mismatch, second screen. The breakdown uses ₹500 and ₹750, but the button says Pay ₹200 and the background selects ₹200; its offer code, GETM200, also differs from the first screen’s GETM500. Shown unchanged, for its information structure. GST and TDS are historical interface labels, not verified tax treatment or savings.

Offer eligibility is a separate question

Explain the condition inside the offer.

The two cards share a structure. The available one carries an Apply action; the unavailable one reduces emphasis but keeps its activation condition inside the card, next to the benefit it qualifies. This is eligibility to apply an offer, separate from the rules that release money already held as locked bonus, which are not shown here.

Sample mismatch. The unavailable offer asks for a ₹500 minimum in its activation message and ₹200 in its description. Shown unchanged.

Tournament discovery 04 / 05

More to decide with. Fewer entries in view.

I brought event state, entry price, capacity and prize information into richer tournament cards. The redesign gives each decision more space; the visible cost is density.

The visible tradeoff.

Information per entry went up; the number of entries in view went down. The brief kept the header as it was, though this pair does not show it unchanged, and no test established that players preferred the larger cards. The “40%” and “Winnings” labels stay uninterpreted.

Delivery 05 / 05

The craft is in the distinctions.

Winnings and bonus. A charge and a headline total. A request received and a payment completed. These were the distinctions I worked on across Mega Poker’s money flows and tournament interfaces.

What the exports establish.

Design scope across the four areas. They do not establish full production coverage or measured impact, and the samples with inconsistent figures stay labelled and unchanged.
