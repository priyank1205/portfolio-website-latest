The starting point

The data was public.
Getting to it took work.

Journalists, researchers and government readers could get the same numbers elsewhere. CEDA’s old portal asked them to log in, configure a chart, then click “Get Visualization” to see it.

At kickoff, CEDA reported around two visitors a day. The brief was to make its dashboards easier to use, bring their visual language together, and help charts reach people beyond the portal.

The workspace

Show the chart first.
Let the controls redraw it.

I made the chart the starting point and organised the controls around it. A change to an indicator, filter or year redraws the visualisation, removing the extra “Get Visualization” step.

The structure holds map, bar and line views at state and district level. The question, selected filters and source stay beside the result.

Clarity lives in the details.

Keep the context where the reader needs it.

The boundary

I chose one non-scrolling workspace at desktop width. That keeps the controls in view, but the same arrangement needs separate validation on small screens.

Behind the workspace decision

“Should not wait for the user to click CTA every time they change some parameter”My first-impressions notes · 8 October 2023

The February 2024 inventory defined twelve parts: navigation, chart and geography selectors, title, selected context, chart, hover, timeline, download, share, heatmap, displayed states and filters.

The taxonomy also needed room to grow: my 4 March note identified nine existing categories against twelve in the data. Units, an India comparison value and legend logic were implementation dependencies.

Comparison

Make the second chart
as easy to find as the first.

Side-by-side comparison already existed. Its entry point was an instruction in the old top bar. I gave it an explicit “Compare Chart” action, then adapted the selection pattern from our earlier Ecometer work.

Search serves readers who know the indicator’s name; categories give everyone else a place to begin.

From the chart

Two questions.
Room for both.

Each chart keeps its own filters and timeline. Linked highlighting follows the same geography across both maps.

The still shows the layout; linked highlighting is part of the shipped behaviour.

The tradeoff

Independent controls make comparison flexible, but allow different years and filters. I would test whether readers can explain a difference while accounting for each chart’s year, unit and scale.

Reuse, feedback and the shipped landing page

“Need a button to compare data - Same design as in ecometer - Popup with browsing as well as search capabilities”CEDA feedback · 8 October 2023

This was intentional reuse of a selection pattern, adapted to SEDP. The earlier Ecometer modal has category and market configuration; the supplied frame has no search field. These frames do not establish shared code.

I also designed a discovery page for the first chart. CEDA’s feedback moved region selection into visible All / District / State controls and gave “Browse charts by source” its own section.

The decision I held onto

The labels were the feature.

The export proved difficult to implement. I had positioned the labels and typography by hand; the values needed to populate from the selected chart. CEDA proposed exporting the heatmap alone.

That would remove the information that made the image readable outside the portal. I researched implementation approaches and worked with the developer until we could build the intended design.

“We have been able to find a way to show exactly how the design looked”My handover email note · 9 April 2024

From design to public use

The format travelled.

CEDA’s posts on 8 and 24 March 2026 use the self-contained export format, almost two years after handover.

The published output includes year and unit in the title, two-decimal values, “Indian Average” and “NA” for missing data. These are visible production differences; I do not attribute those refinements to myself.

The bar and line export formats

What changed

A shipped workspace.
An insight people could share.

Everything I designed for SEDP shipped: the chart workspace, comparison, discovery page and share exports. The March 2026 posts provide dated evidence of the export format’s use.

CEDA told me faculty across Ashoka used the dashboards for research and reported a broader audience through social-media engagement. I had no access to usage data after handover, so I claim no adoption or retention figures.

What I would test next.

Whether readers understand comparisons with different years or filters. Whether a shared chart leads someone back to the data. And whether values remain accessible with a keyboard, without colour, and on a small screen.

The wider CEDA engagement

Related products. Different analytical jobs.

SEDP followed my work on Ecometer and Agrimarket. Both shipped; the design frames below show the specific decisions behind each.

Ecometer

Read economic series, their transformations and their sources.

Ecometer brings a different analytical task into the CEDA work: reading high-frequency economic series. I designed the workspace, series controls, transformations and indicator explanations shown here. All shipped; these are the supplied design mockups.

Reading the selection

A series is more than
a coloured line.

Vegetables, pulses and grains carry individual retail or wholesale labels. The right panel connects each name to its chart colour and exposes visibility and removal controls. Edible Oils remains listed in a faded state: the selection can be inspected even when a series is not prominent.

This keeps three questions together: what is selected, which market it describes, and whether it is visible. The Add Chart modal shown in SEDP’s comparison episode supplies the category and series configuration.

Levels → rates of change

Absolute, yearly,
quarterly or monthly.

The growth control sits beside the indicator title. It distinguishes absolute values from Year on Year, Quarter on Quarter and Month on Month growth, then explains that a relative rate changes the y-axis to a percentage scale.

The useful distinction is between the level of a series and its change over a chosen interval. Keeping that choice visible gives the reader context for interpreting the chart; the supplied frame shows the choice and explanation, rather than a calculated result.

Definition beside the measure

Keep the source
within reach.

The information control opens the indicator’s definition: how CEDA’s retail and wholesale food-price indices relate to the underlying data. A source-portal link and a separate methodology note support a deeper reading without taking over the workspace.

My reading of the tradeoff: exposing selection, transformation and provenance makes analytical choices inspectable, but also asks readers to understand them. These frames do not establish frequency or unit safeguards.

Agrimarket

Keep exact observations beside the bigger pattern.

For agricultural prices and arrivals, I brought the dated records and visual analysis into one workspace. The redesign and its interaction states shipped. The old interface captures and supplied redesign mockups below show the change in organisation.

Before / Separate entry and configuration

Time Series, Heat Map and Data Download already existed. The old entry screen offered separate routes; the supplied configuration state asked for a measure, commodity and time before Submit.

Two old states, not a recording of a navigation sequence or a matched task comparison. Displayed dates do not establish the design chronology.

The selected commodity and geography frame both sides. The table preserves individual dates, quantities and prices; the chart reveals their temporal pattern. Together they connect individual observations to the broader view.

Explain the reference point

“Change” from which price?

The Change-column explanation specifies the last available price, generally the next table row. It makes the reference explicit instead of leaving a reader to assume a consecutive calendar day.

Separate one signal

Let one price line stand out.

The legend-isolation state gives modal price prominence while the other lines recede. The legend remains visible, preserving the meaning of the quieter marks.

Time → geography

Change the view.
Keep the observations.

The alternate map keeps the summary and table beside geographical variation. Highest- and lowest-price cards and the scale provide a readout around the map.

Take a scoped result

Make the download
selection explicit.

The dialog carries commodity, geography and measure into the download step. Period controls and CSV / PNG / PDF choices expose the scope and format; the action names the selected CSV format.

This completes the analytical workflow at the level of configuration. SEDP’s export has a separate job: explaining itself to someone outside the portal.

My reading of the tradeoff: keeping records and patterns together demands space. The desktop mockups do not establish keyboard or touch equivalents, small-screen behaviour, or numerical correctness. The dialog shows configuration; an exported file is not part of the supplied evidence.
