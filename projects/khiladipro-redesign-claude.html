The brief 00 / 05

“Design the olympiad.”

That was the whole brief. Strategy and success metrics stayed with the client, for whom the olympiad was an acquisition play into casual sports participants. The design was mine: four surfaces, shaped by five constraints.

First-run onboarding 01 / 05

The moment the phone leaves your hand

If onboarding fails, the drill cannot be scored, and in a paid event that becomes a refund request. So I designed it as a rehearsal: hold the phone, prop it up, step away, then move. Scroll to walk all nine screens, or step through them yourself.

Where the phone is In the hand
phone across the room
  1. A video introduces camera-based scoring before the app asks for access.
  2. Explain what the camera tracks before the system permission request appears.
  3. The first drill asks the player to fit their face inside an oval.
  4. A placement guide, read while still holding the phone, prepares the player to prop it up and step away.
  5. The phone is down. The second drill asks the player to stand inside a full-body guide.
  6. A single, oversized “Perfect!” confirms the position from across the room.
  7. Six jumping jacks appear as the next instruction, without a trip through a menu.
  8. The third drill combines a body guide, set progress and a large 3/6 counter.
  9. The player walks back and picks the phone up: three drills complete, ten points earned.

Why rehearse at all

Same frame, different stakes

Onboarding does not explain the app. It runs the exact screen the scored test will use, so the first time it counts is the second time you have seen it. Side by side, only the drill and the stakes change.

The frame is locked across both

What it buys

A player’s first scored drill is never their first drill.

The instruction, the body guide and the counter have all been seen once already, with nothing at stake. The first attempt that counts should never fail because the screen was new.

What it costs

Every onboarding step keeps “Skip for now”.

It gives returning users a way through, and also lets a first-timer skip the rehearsal that makes their first drill scoreable. I shipped that trade-off knowingly.

    Unfinished business

    If I picked it back up

    No analytics access meant no impact story. These are the two things I would set up first if the olympiad ran again.

    Measure

    Three funnels, none of which existed.

    • RegistrationVisitor to paid entry, split by audience and by device.
    • First useFirst scoreable drill, onboarding drop-offs, failed attempts.
    • ParticipationReturn to the next test, and unscoreable submissions.

    Test

    In their own rooms, not in a lab.

    • Can they set it up?Position the phone, read feedback while moving, finish a scoreable drill.
    • Can they read it from the mat?How much of the screen, the instruction and the count still land once the phone is on the ground, and how comfortable that angle is to hold for a whole drill.
    • Can they hear the cues?Audio prompts with music playing, with a television in the room, outdoors, and from the far side of the room.
