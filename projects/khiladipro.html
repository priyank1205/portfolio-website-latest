The brief

“Design the olympiad.”

The engagement was execution-focused: strategy and success metrics stayed with the client. My first job was to map that brief into four surfaces: the landing page, registration and payment, first-run onboarding, and the in-app event.

For the client, the olympiad was an acquisition play into casual sports participants adjacent to its core audience of young athletes.

The constraintWhat it forced

Inherited app language

No formal design system; the new screens had to feel native.

Use type, scale and consistent state patterns within the existing visual language.

Fixed school-holiday window

25 May–15 June; marketing needed the pages beforehand.

Give the event dates prominence and make the registration deadline visible.

Three audiences, one URL

Students compete, parents pay and supervise, academies bring cohorts.

Sequence distinct promises on a single landing page.

Payment before installation

Registration and payment happen on the web.

Put reassurance beside the form, before the family downloads the app.

A team of four

One designer, a product manager, an engineer and the CEO.

Own the complete design across the website, onboarding and event screens.

01 / First-run setup

The far side of the room

If setup fails, the drill cannot be scored. In a paid event, that can become a refund request. I designed setup as a rehearsal: hold the phone, prop it up, step away, then move.

When the test counts

From things to tap to things to read while moving.

When the phone leaves the hand, type size replaces tap size as the main concern. The scored test uses the same pattern: a drill instruction, a body guide and a prominent count.

Guidance versus skipping

Every setup step keeps “Skip for now”. It gives familiar users a way through, but also lets a new player bypass the preparation that makes a drill scoreable.

03 / Between attempts

Designing the waiting

An event app is mostly time between attempts. Each state needs to explain where the participant stands and offer a next step, inside the app’s inherited visual language.

Before committing to 24 minutes

The stage sheet puts the estimated time, the drills and peer completion together before starting a test. It makes the commitment visible before the test begins.

Outcome

Shipped as designed.

Everything I designed for KhiladiPro shipped: the web journey, onboarding and event screens. The registration page remains available in production.

After the olympiad, the growth team brought me back for an IPL fan-campaign landing page on desktop and mobile. The in-house team owned that campaign’s app screens.

What I would measure

Registration
Visitor → paid entry, split by audience and device.
First use
First scoreable drill, setup drop-offs and failed attempts.
Participation
Return to the next test and unscoreable submissions.

What I would test Prospective

Watch younger participants set up in their own rooms: can they position the phone, read feedback while moving and finish a scoreable drill? Test parent comprehension of the mobile landing page.

What I would fix

  • The placement copy and illustration disagree; I shipped that inconsistency and would align them.
  • Revisit the binary gender field and establish what the competition actually needs.
  • Add poor-network and failed-upload recovery for drill videos.
