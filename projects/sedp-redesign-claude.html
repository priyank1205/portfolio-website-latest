The starting point 00 / 06

The data was public. Getting to it took work.

Journalists, researchers and government readers could get the same numbers elsewhere. CEDA’s old portal asked them to log in, configure a chart, then click “Get Visualization” to see it.

At kickoff, from my notes of 13 September 2023

Readers
Journalists, researchers and government.
Baseline
About two visitors a day, as CEDA reported it.
Alternatives
NDAP, the India Data Portal, or the data in CSV and Excel.

Where the old portal made readers work

Before any of it

A gate before the value.

An account came before the data. The old portal’s front door was a login page, so a reader had to commit before seeing a single chart. That was the old product; it says nothing about how the redesign is accessed.

The brief

  1. Make the dashboards easier to use.
  2. Bring their visual language together.
  3. Help charts reach people beyond the portal.

The constraints

Brand
CEDA’s existing identity.
Team
Two developers and one department head.
Window

Consistency
A visual language shared with CEDA’s related dashboards.

The workspace 01 / 06

Show the chart first. Let the controls redraw it.

I made the chart the starting point and organised the controls around it. A change to an indicator, filter or year redraws the visualisation, removing the extra “Get Visualization” step. The structure holds map, bar and line views at state and district level.

What the screens don’t show.

Units, an India comparison value and legend logic were implementation dependencies, and the inventory still listed negative values as under consideration.

The boundary I chose.

One non-scrolling workspace at desktop width. That keeps the controls in view, but the same arrangement needs separate validation on small screens.

Comparison 02 / 06

Make the second chart as easy to find as the first.

Side-by-side comparison already existed; its entry point was an instruction in the old top bar. I gave it an explicit “Compare Chart” action, then adapted the selection pattern from our earlier Ecometer work. Search serves readers who know the indicator’s name; categories give everyone else a place to begin.

“Need a button to compare data - Same design as in ecometer - Popup with browsing as well as search capabilities”

CEDA feedback, 8 October 2023

This was intentional reuse of a selection pattern, adapted to SEDP. The frames show the adaptation; they do not establish shared code.

Socio-Economic Data

Two questions, room for both. Each chart keeps its own filters and timeline. Design frame, sample data; linked highlighting across the maps is part of the shipped behaviour, not something this still can show.

The tradeoff.

Independent controls make comparison flexible, but allow different years and filters. I tracked the choice on 8 October 2023 (“Filters and Timeline separate in Compare Charts Flow”), and I would test whether readers can explain a difference while accounting for each chart’s year, unit and scale.

Another view of the same question

Find a state’s position without leaving the workspace.

The bar view ranks every state by name, with Top 20 and Bottom 20 options, so a reader can locate one place in the distribution.

From feedback to the shipped page

A discovery page for the first chart.

CEDA’s feedback moved region selection into visible All, District and State controls, and gave “Browse charts by source” its own section.

The chart that leaves 03 / 06

An exported chart has to explain itself.

A reader on social media has none of the portal’s context. I designed the export as its own surface: the question, the selected filters, labelled values, a readout and a route back to the data.

Three questions it answers alone

The decision I held onto

The labels were the feature

The export proved difficult to implement. I had positioned the labels and typography by hand; the values needed to populate from the selected chart. CEDA proposed exporting the heatmap alone. That would have removed the information that made the image readable outside the portal. I researched implementation approaches and worked with the developer until we could build the intended design.

“We have been able to find a way to show exactly how the design looked”

My handover email note, 9 April 2024

From design to public use

The format travelled

CEDA’s posts on 8 and 24 March 2026 use the self-contained export format, almost two years after handover.

The published output includes the year and unit in its title, two-decimal values, “Indian Average” and “NA” for missing data. These are visible production differences; I do not attribute those refinements to myself.

Earlier CEDA work 04 / 06

Ecometer: series, their transformations and their sources

Ecometer brings a different analytical task into the CEDA work: reading high-frequency economic series. I designed the workspace, series controls, transformations and indicator explanations shown here. The redesign shipped; these are the supplied design mockups.

From indicator, to chart, to selected series

Reading the selection

A series is more than a coloured line.

Vegetables, pulses and grains carry individual retail or wholesale labels. The panel connects each name to its chart colour and exposes visibility and removal. Edible Oils stays listed in a faded state, so the selection can be inspected even when a series is not prominent. Adding a series uses the dialog shown in the comparison chapter.

Levels to rates of change

Change the analytical question.

The growth control sits beside the indicator title. It separates absolute values from Year on Year, Quarter on Quarter and Month on Month growth, then explains that a rate changes the y-axis to a percentage scale. The frame shows the choice and its explanation, not a calculated result.

Definition beside the measure

Keep the source within reach.

The information control opens the indicator’s definition: how CEDA’s retail and wholesale food-price indices relate to the underlying data, with a source-portal link and a methodology note.

My reading of the tradeoff: exposing selection, transformation and provenance makes analytical choices inspectable, but also asks readers to understand them. These frames do not establish frequency or unit safeguards.

Earlier CEDA work 05 / 06

Agrimarket: exact observations beside the bigger pattern

For agricultural prices and arrivals, I brought the dated records and the visual analysis into one workspace. The redesign and its interaction states shipped; the old interface captures and redesign mockups below show the change in organisation.

Before: separate entry and configuration

After: records and patterns together

Time to geography

Change the view. Keep the observations.

The alternate map keeps the summary and table beside geographical variation, with highest and lowest price cards and the scale around the map. The map and the latest table entry show different dates, so this still does not establish live synchronisation.

Take a scoped result

Make the download selection explicit.

The dialog carries commodity, geography and measure into the download, with period controls and CSV, PNG and PDF choices; the action names the selected format. SEDP’s export has a different job: explaining itself to someone outside the portal.

My reading of the tradeoff: keeping records and patterns together demands space. The desktop mockups do not establish keyboard or touch equivalents, small-screen behaviour, or numerical correctness, and an exported file is not part of the evidence.

What changed 06 / 06

A shipped workspace. An insight people could share.

Everything I designed for SEDP shipped: the chart workspace, comparison, discovery page and share exports. The March 2026 posts give dated evidence of the export format in use. CEDA told me faculty across Ashoka used the dashboards for research, and reported a broader audience through social media. I had no access to usage data after handover, so I claim no adoption or retention figures.

For SEDP, before anything else.
