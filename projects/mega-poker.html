I designed the decisions around adding, moving and withdrawing money inside an existing poker product.

From a balance in the wallet to a request in progress, the interface had to explain what the money meant.

Withdrawal & self-transfer

The amount is only
half the decision.

A player choosing to withdraw needs to understand the limits, the net amount and the alternative being offered. I brought those choices into the flow, before the request is submitted.

The transfer offer encourages keeping winnings in the product. It is a different destination for the money, not the same outcome as cashing out.

The hierarchy has a bias

The transfer action is more prominent than “No. I will withdraw ₹800.” A visible decline does not establish equal choice.

Transfer is selected in this example; an initial default is not established. The separate ₹45 offer is not included in the ₹900 total. Bonus restrictions, reversibility and the complete route after declining remain unverified.

The offer across contexts

Same incentive.
Different money decisions.

The bonus offer appears at table exit, beside winnings in the wallet, and within withdrawal. I designed its presentation across those contexts, keeping principal and bonus as separate balances.

The transfer contexts

Table exit · Wallet · Amount selection

Separate examples with different balances, not one continuous transaction. These screens establish placement and hierarchy; they do not establish who proposed the offer moments or their effect on behaviour.

Deposits

a single balance.

“Pay ₹500, get ₹750” combines deposit money and locked bonus. I designed the amount selection and breakdown to separate the charge, the adjustments and each destination.

The distinction is visible in the summary. The exports do not establish whether every player opened or understood it before paying.

Tournament discovery

More to decide with.
Fewer entries in view.

I brought event state, entry price, capacity and prize information into richer tournament cards. The redesign gives the decision more space.

The visible cost is density: roughly five compact rows in the old reference, compared with one full card and part of the next in the design.

The visible tradeoff

Information per entry increased; the number of entries visible together decreased. This is a layout comparison, not a tested preference for the redesign.

The old reference is Freeroll and already shows time and participant counts. The surrounding headers also differ. The redesign’s “40%” and “Winnings” labels remain uninterpreted because their meaning is unconfirmed.

Reflection

The craft is in
the distinctions.

Winnings and bonus. A charge and a headline total. A request received and a payment completed.

These were the distinctions I worked on across Mega Poker’s money flows and tournament interfaces. The selected exports establish design scope; they do not establish full production coverage or measured impact.

Scope and evidence notes

The brief also requested UPI, penny-check verification, fee and speed variants, and a TDS explanation sheet. The selected exports do not establish delivery of every branch. Historical samples with inconsistent figures remain labelled and unchanged.
