The core interaction

When the model changes,
the first move changes.

The business model changed what it meant to start a game. I was repeatedly given the core flow each new model needed.

The model I joined

Play until the tournament closes.

Players paid gems to enter time-bounded casual-game tournaments, played as often as they liked before close, and the top three split the pool. The product was not near product–market fit.

On-demand matchmaking

Wait for a game to fill.

Players searched for opponents, pooled their entry fees and played synchronously. The winner took the pool. I designed the path from finding a match to seeing the result.

From finding a match to seeing the resultFour stages · Late 2019

Late 2019 · Final Figma designs that shipped.

We ran Casual, Card and Trivia. Trivia worked best; the others less so.

The structural constraint: enough compatible players had to be searching at the same moment.

Cash-table entry

Join a game
already in progress.

That liquidity problem, and a market where most Indian real-money spend went to poker and rummy, led to a different format.

About six seats. Sit with money, join from the next hand, play for stakes with rake per hand, leave when you like. I designed the sit flow and buy-in.

From flow to interaction

Keep the amount
above the thumb.

On press, the value label lifts above the slider. The amount stays readable while the player adjusts the buy-in.

Cash tables became the main product and revenue format for most of my tenure, across poker, rummy and casual games. Trivia was retired from the cash-table format.

Added alongside cash tables

Tournaments brought
players to the tables.

Once cash tables were the core, we added multi-table tournaments (MTTs). They drew strong participation and funnelled players into cash tables.

I designed the tournament surface, including a details page with a state for each stage. Those states—and keeping players in their hands—are the next part of the story.

Keeping the player in the hand

A way back when it’s your turn.

Poker is turn-based; a missed turn folds the hand. Every surface a player might be looking at instead of the table needs a way back that fires when it is their turn.

Multi-table concept

Carry that return path
between tables.

A floating bubble calls out a turn; the currently-playing sheet brings the player’s games together.

This exact UI stayed a concept; a simpler multi-tabling feature shipped later.

Reasons to return

Progress today.
A reason to return tomorrow.

I watched players stream on YouTube and Twitch, followed how they moved between products, and played extensively across real-money platforms. Players went where the tournaments were good and the opponents beatable.

The product team’s response was a loyalty programme of gameplay-tied tasks. I designed it end to end.

A second mechanism

Scratch cards with
a place and a time.

I conceptualised the scratch-card system: tiers, unlock times and expiry, with a card awarded at the deposit-success moment.

Weekday DAU was around 15k during this period and was later observed around 25k—a team/product outcome not separable from other changes happening around the same time.

2021 · Growth experiment

How the work ran

Holding the context
as the product changed.

I joined as the second product designer. Design reviews with the CEO ran daily. As the design team grew, I helped run the hiring pipeline. For most of those two years, I held the product-to-engineering context for the flows I designed.

Outcome and limits

What shipped.
What I took forward.

Designed and shipped

  • On-demand matchmaking, from search to results.
  • Cash-table sit flow, buy-in, wallet and history.
  • MTT surfaces in portrait and landscape.
  • The contest-card state system.
  • Loyalty tasks, milestones and scratch cards.

The DAU observation describes the product’s scale during this period. It does not isolate the effect of these features or my work.

What I’d do differently

  • Build the component system earlier. The thirteen-state card came late.
  • Brief retention and responsible-play guardrails together. They belong in the same design problem.
  • Write decisions down as we make them. Too much of the context lived in my head.

In 2024, Mega Poker’s CEO, formerly Getmega’s Growth Head, contacted me directly. Mega Poker was a different, restructured company with a different CEO. I worked on its money flows for a few months on a monthly retainer.
