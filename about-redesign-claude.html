I chose interaction design because it sits at the intersection of psychology and technology, two lifelong curiosities. What excites me isn’t making interfaces prettier: it’s understanding how people think, what businesses need, and where technology is headed, then connecting those dots into products people genuinely enjoy using.

Lately that means AI-native building: prototypes become products faster, and designers become builders.

How I work Three values, three episodes

Design with empathy. Think like a product owner. Build with curiosity.

Three values are easy to claim. These are the moments in the case studies where each one had to do real work.

  1. 01Design with empathy

    A rehearsal before the drill that counts

    A teenager props a phone up, steps back and is scored by its camera, in an event a parent paid for. If onboarding failed, the drill could not be scored. So I designed onboarding as a rehearsal on the exact screen the scored test uses: the first time it counts is the second time they have seen it.

    In the KhiladiPro case study

  2. 02Think like a product owner

    The labels were the feature

    The share export proved hard to build, and CEDA proposed exporting the heatmap alone. That would have removed what made the image readable outside the portal. I researched implementation approaches and worked with the developer until we could build it. Almost two years after handover, CEDA’s March 2026 posts use it.

    In the SEDP case study

  3. 03Build with curiosity

    Research by playing

    At Getmega, I watched players stream on YouTube and Twitch, followed how they moved between products, and played across real-money platforms myself. Players went where the tournaments were good and the opponents beatable. The product team answered with a loyalty programme of gameplay-tied tasks, and I designed it end to end.

    In the Getmega case study
